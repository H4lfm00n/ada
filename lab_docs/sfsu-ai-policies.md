## Can I use AI tools like ChatGPT on my homework?

According to SFSU's Office of Student Conduct, using AI counts as plagiarism or cheating
under the CSU Student Conduct Code unless your instructor specifically allows it for that
assignment or in the syllabus. Ask your instructor about each assignment. This kiosk
can't give permission or decide what's allowed in a class.

Source: Academic Integrity, Office of Student Conduct, conduct.sfsu.edu/academic-integrity-faculty (undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## SFSU's advice for students using AI

SFSU's AI for Students page advises students to ask their professor about AI use in a
course, check the syllabus and each assignment, double-check AI output against other
sources, keep sensitive information out of AI tools, and disclose or cite significant AI
help. The page says student AI guidelines are still being developed.

Source: AI for Students, ai.sfsu.edu/ai-students (undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## Does having a campus AI account mean AI is allowed in every class?

No. According to SFSU's AI for Students page, having access to a campus AI tool doesn't
make it allowed in a class. The professor, the syllabus, and each assignment decide.

Source: AI for Students, ai.sfsu.edu/ai-students (undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## Who handles academic integrity cases at SFSU

The Office of Student Conduct handles alleged academic integrity violations. According to
its page, students get notice and a chance to respond. The office is in SSB 402, at
conduct@sfsu.edu and 415-338-2032.

Source: Academic Integrity, Office of Student Conduct, conduct.sfsu.edu/academic-integrity-faculty (undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## AI statements in SFSU syllabi

According to SFSU's teaching center, CEETL,
SFSU's course syllabus policy, S25-257, requires every syllabus to include a statement on
how and why generative AI tools can and cannot be used. CEETL's Teaching with Generative
AI page has guides and examples for instructors.

## SFSU guidance for faculty and staff using AI

SFSU's generative AI guidelines for employees say not to put sensitive or FERPA-protected
university data into AI tools, including campus-licensed ones. The guidelines describe
themselves as guidance that points to existing policies, not a policy document.

Source: Generative AI guidelines for SFSU employees, ai.sfsu.edu (undated; introduced 2025-03-03; checked 2026-10-29).
