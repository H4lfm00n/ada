## Using AI on SFSU coursework

SFSU's Office of Student Conduct says that AI use is a violation of the CSU Student Conduct
Code, as plagiarism or cheating, unless the instructor specifically directs it as part of
the assignment or syllabus. Only the instructor can give that permission for a course.

Source: Academic Integrity | Office of Student Conduct, conduct.sfsu.edu/academic-integrity-faculty (undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## SFSU's advice to students on AI

SFSU's AI for Students page advises students to ask the professor about AI use in each
course, check the syllabus and each assignment, verify AI output against other sources,
avoid entering sensitive information, and disclose and cite significant AI contributions.
The page says student-use guidelines are still being developed.

Source: AI for Students, ai.sfsu.edu/ai-students (undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## SFSU Office of Student Conduct

SFSU's Office of Student Conduct handles alleged academic integrity violations. According
to its page, a student gets notice and a chance to respond. Contact: conduct@sfsu.edu,
415-338-2032, SSB 402.

Source: Academic Integrity | Office of Student Conduct, conduct.sfsu.edu/academic-integrity-faculty (undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## AI statements in SFSU syllabi

According to SFSU's CEETL,
SFSU Course Syllabus Policy S25-257 requires every syllabus to include a statement of how
and why generative AI tools can and cannot be used in the course.

Source: Teaching with Generative AI, ceetl.sfsu.edu/teaching-generative-ai (page undated; linked resources Fall 2025 to April 2026; checked 2026-10-29).

## CEETL guidance for teaching with AI

CEETL's guidance for instructors is to communicate transparently with students, teach about
AI and its limits, use AI where it is educationally appropriate, and protect learning from
shortcuts. CEETL calls its "Three Laws" a framework, not literal rules, and this guidance is
not a new binding policy. CEETL links a teaching guide (updated Fall 2025), faculty
approaches (updated Spring 2026), and AI teaching scholarship (updated April 2026).

Source: Teaching with Generative AI, ceetl.sfsu.edu/teaching-generative-ai (page undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## ChatGPT Edu in assignments

SFSU's ChatGPT Edu page says instructors who require ChatGPT Edu for an assignment or
assessment should offer an alternative, because the page reports accessibility barriers.

Source: OpenAI ChatGPT Edu, ai.sfsu.edu/chatgpt-edu (undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## SFSU generative AI guidelines for employees

SFSU's generative AI guidelines for employees apply to staff, administrators, and faculty.
The guidelines say they are not a policy document: they highlight existing CSU and SFSU
policies, and they don't set student or classroom rules. SFSU introduced them on March 3,
2025.

Source: Generative AI guidelines for SFSU employees, ai.sfsu.edu/index.php/generative-ai-guidelines-sfsu-employees (undated, checked 2026-10-29); Introducing the Generative AI Guidelines, ai.sfsu.edu/news/introducing-generative-ai-guidelines-sf-state-employees-0 (source date 2025-03-03, checked 2026-10-29).

## AI and university data

SFSU's employee guidelines say not to enter or upload sensitive or FERPA-related university
data into AI tools, including campus-licensed AI. Proposals involving sensitive data or
confidential or critical processes go to the IT provider and the appropriate administrator.
Employees should check AI output for accuracy and bias, keep human responsibility,
acknowledge significant AI contributions, and not upload copyrighted work without the
owner's permission.

Source: Generative AI guidelines for SFSU employees, ai.sfsu.edu/index.php/generative-ai-guidelines-sfsu-employees (undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## AI and SFSU branding

SFSU's employee guidelines say AI-generated assets should not represent the University's
brand. SFSU's Working with AI page says public-facing AI-assisted communications should
follow SFSU's branding and editorial guidance. The guidelines link CSU Responsible Use,
SFSU Confidential Data, Academic Integrity policy S22-298, and the Spring 2024 AI
resolution.

Source: Generative AI guidelines for SFSU employees, ai.sfsu.edu/index.php/generative-ai-guidelines-sfsu-employees; Working with AI, ai.sfsu.edu/index.php/working-ai (both undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## Can I use ChatGPT on my homework?

According to SFSU's Office of Student Conduct, AI use on coursework is only allowed when the
instructor directs it in the assignment or syllabus. Ask your instructor about the specific
assignment; only the instructor can give that permission.

Source: Academic Integrity | Office of Student Conduct, conduct.sfsu.edu/academic-integrity-faculty (undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## Does a campus AI account mean AI is allowed in every class?

No. Having a campus AI account doesn't give permission to use AI in a class. SFSU's AI for
Students page directs students to the professor, the syllabus, and each assignment.

Source: AI for Students, ai.sfsu.edu/ai-students (undated, checked 2026-10-29).

## Can I upload student records to a campus-licensed AI tool?

No. SFSU's employee guidelines say sensitive or FERPA-related university data should not
be uploaded to AI tools, including campus-licensed ones. Ask the appropriate IT provider or
administrator.

Source: Generative AI guidelines for SFSU employees, ai.sfsu.edu/index.php/generative-ai-guidelines-sfsu-employees (undated, checked 2026-10-29).
